How it happened

From an idea to 600 kits

A local chain of research, production, verification, volunteer work, technology and community partnerships.

July–August
Idea and fundraising

Donors in Europe and Creative Commons Venezuela set a concrete target of 600 kits.

August
Design and suppliers

We compare dimensions, prices and availability and reject unsuitable materials.

August–September
Local production and UCV team

Six hundred pencil cases are made in Venezuela while students receive and verify supplies.

September
Platform and QR codes

A privacy-conscious system connects each kit with one optional drawing.

September–October
Assembly

All materials arrive and the team completes 600 of 600 kits.

3–10 October
Delivery and response

509 kits reach children and 60 drawings return through the QR invitations.

The larger programme

What happens after kit number 600?

The first batch proves that the model can work. Now we want to complete the creative cycle and prepare a new batch before the end of the year.

🎨

Drawings, jury and prizes

Engage families, form a diverse jury and select ten drawings across ages and experiences.

🖼️

A public exhibition

With explicit permission, create a digital and travelling exhibition without exposing children’s identities.

🔁

A repeatable programme

Reconnect donors, students, local producers, organisations and communities, improving every cycle.

Help us prepare the next batch

The first 600 kits are funded and assembled. New contributions will support the prizes, documentation and the next production cycle.
